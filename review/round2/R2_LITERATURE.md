## Notes per topic

**1. PR bias.** Treat the null-model expectation g₀(n,D)=n/(n+D) as the baseline for every measured g. It is the iid-Gaussian value and holds closely: simulated values at n/D ∈ {1/64, 1/16, 1/4, 1, 4} are within 0.5% of it. When n ≪ D, g ≈ n/D grows linearly in n, so g is a sample-size clock under the null. Chun et al. (ICLR 2026) derive unbiased estimators of the PR numerator and denominator, and on LLM activations their corrected estimate is invariant to sample size. Use the ratio g/g₀ or their estimator. The uncentered second moment adds a rank-1 mean component that lowers PR independently of n (see 9). Related work: Recanatesi et al. 2022 (Patterns, "A scale-dependent measure of system dimensionality") **V** (title and venue only).

**2. Regimes.** Biroli et al. find three regimes: (i) Brownian, before speciation; (ii) speciation at t_S=½ log Λ; (iii) collapse onto training points at t_C, which needs memorisation and is not relevant to large text-to-image models. Λ, and therefore t_S, grows with resolution: t_S rises from 2.8 for CIFAR to 3.1 for ImageNet32. That matches the resolution-dependent timestep shift in SD3 (Esser et al. 2024, ICML, arXiv 2403.03206 **K**). For rectified-flow latent models, expect class commitment at RF time close to 1, after the shift. Biroli et al. report that at finite size the speciation crossover is smooth over Δt≈O(1), roughly a factor of e² ≈ 7 in SNR. A gradual telemetry ramp does not contradict the theory. Sclocchi et al. sharpen this: high-level features flip abruptly, while low-level features change smoothly throughout. Averaged telemetry mixes the two and so looks gradual.

**3. Content vs details.** P2's SNR bands are the most citable numeric anchors: coarse below 10⁻², content from 10⁻² to 10⁰, details above 10⁰. Guidance-interval results put the semantically useful window at σ≈0.2–3 with σ_data=0.5. eDiff-I is the original evidence that one denoiser behaves differently across noise levels (text matters early). Falck et al. argue that this coarse-to-fine ordering comes from the frequency-dependent SNR and is not essential to diffusion.

**4. Distillation.** No teacher–student RSA or CKA study at matched σ was found, and this is a gap in the literature. Closest evidence: Gandikota & Bau show that concept directions transfer from base to distilled model, so representational structure is preserved. They also show distilled models commit to structure at step 1 ("diversity collapse"). Z-Image-Turbo was trained without CFG at inference, using CFG-augmented DMD. Because both models get σ through the timestep embedding, σ-locked telemetry should match "for free" (see Gan et al. in 9). Telemetry that depends on content state at equal σ may differ, because the Turbo trajectory is already committed.

**5. Similarity pitfalls.** Depth profiles are smooth and share architecture-generic shapes: norm growth, a mid-layer intrinsic-dimension hump (Cheng et al. 2025), and late-layer collapse. A correlation of r≈0.8 between unrelated models is therefore an expected baseline, not evidence of shared computation. Use layer-permutation or IAAFT-style surrogate nulls, detrended or first-differenced profiles, and debiased CKA.

**6. Synchrony artefacts.** The Yule and Granger–Newbold "nonsense correlation" between trending series is the classic artefact. Surrogate testing: Theiler 1992 for phase-randomised surrogates, Schreiber & Schmitz 1996/2000 for IAAFT, Thiel et al. 2006 for twin surrogates aimed at synchronisation. Lancaster et al. 2018 is a good review. The Kuramoto order parameter R=|⟨e^{iφ}⟩| assumes phases, so applying it to z-scored monotone series is ill-posed. No literature states the z-scoring mid-run minimum directly. The simulation supports it as an interior minimum whose position depends on curve shape, so cite it as an internal null rather than as literature.

**7. ComfyUI.** Verified from `comfy/samplers.py` (raw GitHub, master). The batch-size decision uses free memory with a 1.5× safety factor, so the same workflow can run cond and uncond together or separately on different machines or at different times. No GitHub issue was found that documents nondeterminism from this.

**8. Abliteration.** The direction comes from a single layer l*, but it is projected out of every residual-writing matrix in every layer. For mostly harmless telemetry prompts, the activation change ≈ (projection onto r), which is small. Any spectral change should appear only where activations have a component along r, which grows in the middle layers. The ≈0.8L cutoff comes from the appendix and was only partly verified **?**.

**9. Massive activations.** With an uncentered second moment, s₁/Σs is dominated by the mean, the BOS/sink token and the massive-activation channels, both in LLMs and in DiTs (SD3, SD3.5, Flux). In DiTs these are timestep-modulated (Gan et al. 2025). Recommend reporting the spectral gap with BOS/sink tokens removed and after mean-centering, to separate "content dimensionality" from the "bias direction".

**10. Seeds.** There is strong evidence that the initial noise sets layout and position (Ban et al., Mao et al.) and even style and quality (Xu et al.). InitNO and Golden Noise optimise the initial noise for this reason. Together with Raya & Ambrogioni's early linear phase (small perturbations are reverted at the highest noise), this suggests the seed's influence is carried by low-frequency noise structure that survives the linear phase and is amplified at speciation.
