# Mode-I final outline — empirical paper

## Working thesis
Across heterogeneous generative systems, coarse spectral organization and prompt-relational geometry recur, but the most discriminative evidence of inherited computation lies in registered local dynamics. The empirical paper maps which observables survive changes in content, capture regime, architecture, inference settings and distillation, and records the negative results that prevent stronger universality claims.

## 1. Introduction
- Motivation: internal generative trajectories are usually compared through outputs or static representations; here the object is the evolution of layerwise spectral geometry during generation.
- State the empirical questions, not the universal answer in advance.
- Define the release boundary: no separatrix/commitment, no geometry→quality, no context-invariant AR phase claim, no RG/universality-class claim.
- Contributions:
  1. multi-model telemetry atlas;
  2. low-dimensional depth-time organization;
  3. semantic information in ordered depth geometry;
  4. broad cross-model prompt-relational geometry;
  5. exceptionally strong Base↔Turbo tangent inheritance;
  6. separate sigma-axis and layer-axis phase/topology observations;
  7. estimator and synchronization falsification results.

## 10. Empirical synthesis
- Coarse organization can recur across architectures.
- Prompt relational geometry is broadly shared.
- Local tangent dynamics are far more lineage-specific.
- The same data therefore support a hierarchy of invariance, not one universal microscopic law.
- Precisely list unresolved new-data gates.
