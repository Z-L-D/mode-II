**Implications (Topic 1).**
- **Encoder sharing by pair.** ZIT↔ZIB share a byte-identical Qwen3-4B. SD35M↔SD35L share CLIP-L, OpenCLIP-bigG and T5-v1.1-XXL (the same named checkpoints; byte identity not checked). **Mochi shares T5-v1.1-XXL with both SD3.5 models.** Z-Image shares no encoder with Mochi or SD3.5. So the "8 unrelated pairs" are not all equally unrelated at the text-input level: Mochi×SD35M and Mochi×SD35L share one encoder. S2d/S2e-style encoder-order predictions should use three tiers: identical (ZIT/ZIB), full set shared (SD35M/L), partial T5 share (Mochi/SD3.5), none (Z-Image × others).
- **Lineage wording.** The paper says Turbo was distilled from the "foundational SFT model". The released Z-Image card does not say it is that exact checkpoint, and Turbo was released two months earlier. Write "same family; Turbo is a Decoupled-DMD (+RL) distillation of a Z-Image SFT model", not "student of the released ZIB checkpoint".
- **CFG asymmetry.** ZIB uses CFG (3–5) and 28–50 steps. ZIT uses no CFG, 8 NFEs, and CFG distilled into the weights (1.6). Matched-σ comparisons must state whether ZIB activations come from the cond branch, the uncond branch or both.

**Implications (Topic 2).**
- **Prompt-permutation Mantel tests H0: "no association between the RDMs".** It does not test "no association beyond the shared input". With 10 theme domains, both RDMs carry block structure inherited from the prompts. That is the Cui et al. (2.10) confound, and it inflates even random-network RSA. Report RSA against a text-only RDM (TF-IDF and, better, each model's own encoder-output RDM) and a deconfounded or partial RSA. Do not rely on partial Mantel p-values alone (2.7).
- **Restrict permutations within theme** (permute prompts only within the 10 domains) to test similarity beyond theme structure. Calibrate against a permutation null as in 2.9.
- **Encoder sharing is the main alternative explanation** for the ordering of first-step RSA, especially at high σ, where the latent carries almost no prompt information and the text tokens are the only prompt-dependent input (§1).
- **Cite the platonic hypothesis cautiously.** 2.9 shows that its global-metric evidence is largely a scale artifact once calibrated.

**Implications (Topic 3).**
- **Name the metric precisely.** "Amplitude PR" (Σσ)²/Σσ² is not the neuroscience PR, (Σλ)²/Σλ² = (Σσ²)²/Σσ⁴. Both are Rényi-2-type counts but on different spectra, and amplitude PR is always ≥ eigenvalue PR. The amplitude convention matches Roy & Vetterli's p_k ∝ σ_k, so cite 3.1 for the amplitude normalization and 3.3/3.5 only as "related (eigenvalue) PR". Treat g, H and s1/s2 as three functionals of one spectrum, not as independent evidence.
- **Uncentered moments and massive activations.** With an uncentered matrix, s1 is dominated by the mean direction and massive-activation channels. In DiTs these are AdaLN-linked and timestep-modulated (3.11, 3.12). A shared σ-dependent envelope of g or s1/s2 across unrelated DiTs (the S1b "generic envelope") is therefore expected from architecture and conditioning, without any shared content geometry. Report a centered or mean-removed control, or exclude the massive-activation channels, before interpreting the L0 or L1 levels.
- **Similar depth profiles are generic.** 3.7 shows such profiles recur across different transformers, and 3.9 shows that scalar coincidence is uninformative. This supports the observable-hierarchy framing (L0 uninformative, L3 discriminative).

**Implications (Topic 4).** The theory papers (4.1–4.6) describe transitions in the **data distribution and score along the trajectory**, not in network activations. Internal activation statistics vs σ are not, by themselves, evidence of a phase transition. A peak or kink in g(σ) can arise from a smooth unimodal trade-off (4.7) or from timestep-embedding modulation (3.12, 4.9). Claim alignment with speciation only with an external, output-level marker, such as class or layout commitment measured as in 4.4–4.6. I found no published paper that measures PR or effective rank of DiT activations per layer and per step on Z-Image, Mochi or SD3.5. Phrase novelty claims cautiously ("to our knowledge").

**Implications (Topic 5).**
- **Phase-randomized and circular-shift surrogates are invalid here.** Our per-layer series over σ are strongly monotone, which violates the stationarity those nulls assume (5.1, 5.8). Harris (5.8) found that exactly these surrogates still produce false positives.
- **Peaks can come from drift alone.** If each unit's series is z-scored and the series share a monotone drift, an across-unit coherence index (mean pairwise correlation, or the variance explained by PC1) is high wherever the drifts align. A windowed index can peak simply where the slopes are most similar or the residual variance is lowest. Yule (5.6) and Granger–Newbold (5.7) give the mechanism.
- **Use better nulls.** Use trend-preserving nulls (small-shuffle, 5.4; local-mean-preserving, 5.5), or better, the analogue of Harris's session permutation: permute independent units of replication, such as prompts, seeds or models, rather than time.
- **Time-warp surrogates.** I found no peer-reviewed reference that defines "time-warp surrogates" under that name. If we use them, describe them as our own construction.
