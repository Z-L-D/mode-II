## How else to read the old Mochi/ZIB result
1. **Common solver/denoising scaffold:** g is an effective-rank-like scalar. Different architectures can share the same contraction/re-expansion envelope without sharing a local vector field.
2. **Projection degeneracy:** two high-dimensional trajectories can have nearly identical scalar projections while moving in different directions in the discarded coordinates. The tangent result empirically demonstrates this.
3. **Architecture versus lineage split:** Mochi/ZIB coarse resemblance points to cross-architecture recurrence; ZIB/ZIT tangent/semantic inheritance points to lineage. These are complementary, not competing, results.
4. **Capture-functional sensitivity:** the historical ZIB comparison used a pooled/branch-mean regime while ZIT is cond-only and Mochi branch-mean. Round 5 proves pooling can move g on an identical trajectory. Therefore the exact 0.9405 versus 0.9325 ordering should be presented as historical/coarse and not as a precise architecture ranking until raw branch-matched replay is available.
5. **Semantic hierarchy:** ordered depth geometry contains theme information in every tested diffusion model, but backbone-subtracted theme directions are strongly lineage-specific. So 'themes are represented' and 'themes are represented in the same direction' are different claims.
6. **Mode-II implication:** a single universal microscopic F(z) is not supported. A better decomposition is a coarse recurrent organization plus model/lineage-specific depth-time law and content residuals.

## Round-5 estimator correction
Raw sigma50 is not paper-safe across unequal sample sizes. In the archive it moves from roughly 0.72 to 0.94 as n increases; fold partition alone also causes large swings. Relative onset is much more stable. VR also requires calibration: a simulated random walk gives VR4≈0.82, AR(0.8)≈0.72, iid≈0.25. Paper tables should use these calibrated references rather than a universal VR=1 walk baseline.
